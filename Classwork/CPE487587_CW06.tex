Consider a random variable $X \sim Lognormal(\mu, \sigma^2)$ with the probability density function (pdf):

\begin{align*}
p(x) = \frac{1}{x\sigma \sqrt{2\pi}} \exp \bigg( - \frac{(\ln x - \mu)^2}{2\sigma^2} \bigg), \quad x > 0
\end{align*}

and normal distribution $Y\sim \Ncal (m, v^2)$ with pdf

\begin{align*}
q(x) = \frac{1}{v\sqrt{2\pi}} \exp \bigg( -\frac{(x-m)^2}{2v^2}  \bigg), \quad x\in \mathbb{R}
\end{align*}

The KL divergence formula is 

\begin{align*}
KL(p || q) = \int_0^\infty p(x) \log \frac{p(x)}{q(x)}dx
\end{align*}

which is defined only if the support of $P$ is contained in the support of $Q$. This is very important.

Noting the above, compute the KL divergence $KL(Lognormal(\mu, \sigma^2) ||  \Ncal (m, v^2)))$.
